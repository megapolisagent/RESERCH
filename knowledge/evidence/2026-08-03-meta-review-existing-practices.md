# Evidence log — existing self-correction mechanisms (Retrospective/AAR/Double-Loop/OODA/Kaizen/Scientific self-correction/Prior Art)

Date: 2026-08-03. Researched before designing any "Self-Audit" mechanism, per explicit correction: check for an existing analog before inventing. Six practices examined.

## After Action Review (AAR)
Source: US Army FM 7-0 / TC 7-0.1. A formal, scheduled (not ad hoc) 4-step process — Plan, Execute, Evaluate, Integrate and share lessons — built into every training event's cadence, explicitly stated to "signal the start of the next planning cycle." Compares what was *supposed* to happen against what *did* happen, via guided self-discovery (facilitator asks open questions, doesn't lecture), then asks how to do it differently next time. Key structural feature: it is scheduled as part of the activity itself, not invoked only when someone external asks for it.

## Double-Loop Learning (Argyris & Schön, 1974/1978)
Source: infed.org, actiondesign.com, Adams (2023) review. Single-loop learning: detect and correct an error by changing actions while leaving the governing variables (values, rules, framing) untouched — "like a thermostat." Double-loop learning: correct the error by first examining and revising the governing variables themselves, then the actions. This is the precise, named distinction for the gap identified: this project's Evidence Review is single-loop (checks whether a *rule* held on a case, adjusts within the existing framework); nothing has been double-loop (questioning whether *PRINCIPLES.md/METHODOLOGY.md's own governing structure* needs revision). Argyris's own finding: people are "skilfully incompetent" at double-loop learning on their own — it usually requires an external intervener precisely because the governing variables are held tacitly and defended by "organizational defensive routines."

## OODA loop (Boyd)
Source: Richards (Boyd's OODA Loop), strategyu.co, tomorrowdesk.com. Orientation — the mental model through which observation happens — is the center of gravity, not one step in a cycle; it filters what can even be perceived next. The concrete, actionable practice cited for improving orientation over time: **explicit hypothesis tracking** — state the current mental model/expectation before a decision, then after the outcome, compare predicted vs. actual. The *gap* between prediction and outcome is "the direct evidence of orientation error." Without this explicit tracking, "orientation improvement is impossible because failures are attributed to external factors rather than model inadequacy."

## Toyota Kaizen / Standard Work
Source: artoflean.com, leanblog.org. "Where there is no standard, there can be no kaizen" (Ohno) — improvement requires a stable, documented baseline to detect deviation from. Critically: the same people who follow a standard must be the ones who revise it, or the improvement loop breaks down. And: **an unchanged standard for months is itself treated as a management failure, not a sign of stability** — "if standard work has not changed in months, it usually means no improvement is happening."

## Scientific self-correction (Merton, Popper, Allchin 2024)
Source: Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Allchin (2024) "When is Science Self-Correcting." Important, honest caveat: self-correction in science is *not* a guaranteed, automatic mechanism — Allchin's 2024 analysis explicitly states no general mechanism has been identified; it works only under contingent conditions (apt, timely, probative evidence) and depends on Mertonian norms — universalism, communality/openness, disinterestedness, and *organized skepticism*, which Merton describes as requiring **diversity of perspectives** as an enabling condition. Single-actor self-review is structurally weaker than review involving genuinely different perspectives.

## Prior art search (patent law / engineering design)
Source: USPTO's 7-step search guide, Stanford OTL, CASRAI. A mandatory, formalized practice in every mature engineering/invention discipline: before committing to building or filing, systematically search existing patents and non-patent literature (brainstorm terms → keyword search → classification search → review results → follow citation trails), explicitly to avoid "reinventing the wheel," and the search itself is documented as part of the record. This is the strongest, most directly transferable grounding for a general "prove non-existence before inventing" rule — it is not agent-specific or research-specific, it is a decades-old, cross-domain professional norm.

## Synthesis
The gap this project actually has is a missing **double-loop** review layer: Evidence Review (already built) is single-loop by Argyris's exact definition. The correct fix is not a new invented mechanism ("Self-Audit") but an explicit second layer in `REVIEW_LOOP.md`, adapted directly from Double-Loop Learning, using AAR's built-in-cadence discipline (scheduled, not ad hoc) and OODA's explicit-prediction-vs-outcome tracking as the concrete operational technique — with an honest, cited caveat from the scientific self-correction literature that single-agent double-loop review has real, documented limits and should not be presented as a replacement for external review, only a supplement to it.
