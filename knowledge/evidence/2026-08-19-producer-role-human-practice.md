## 1. Real, documented practices of the producer/integrating role — and does the "whole picture first, then functional breakdown" sequence hold

Five independently-sourced role-families found, each disclosed at the mechanism level, not just named — well above the brief's 2–3 minimum:

**EOS Integrator (and the paired Visionary role)** — Gino Wickman's Entrepreneurial Operating System, *Traction* and *Rocket Fuel* (Established, single-methodology-source for the mechanism itself; independently practitioner-corroborated for real-world use — see disagreement section below). The Integrator is described, verbatim, by EOS Worldwide's own site as: "the tie-breaker for the leadership team... is accountable for the P&L results, executes the business plan, holds the Leadership Team accountable... is the filter for all of the Visionary's ideas." The Accountability Chart — the tool that performs the functional decomposition — is explicitly built top-down: "the Integrator sits above the three major functions. The Visionary sits above or beside the Integrator." Sales/scale claimed by the vendor: 190,000+ organizations, 700+ implementers, 2M+ books sold (self-reported by EOS Worldwide, not independently audited — flagged as a claim, not verified).

**Scaling Up / Rockefeller Habits (Verne Harnish)** — a second, independent, competing methodology (Gazelles/Scaling Up explicitly notes it predates EOS). Its Function Accountability Chart (FACe) does the same decomposition move as EOS's chart but is explicit about tying it to actual financial statements: "Take your Profit and Loss (P/L), Balance Sheet (B/S), and Cash Flow accounting statements and assign a person to each line item, then derive appropriate Results/Outcomes for each function."

**COO / "number two" role — academic research (Bennett & Miles)** — genuinely independent of both business-coaching methodologies above: a peer-adjacent HBR article (2006) and a Stanford University Press book, based on first-person interviews across real companies (Xerox, Cisco, Delta, Regus, and others named in the 2016 preface). Their finding: the COO "is typically the key individual responsible for delivery of results day to day, quarter to quarter... plays a critical leadership role in executing the strategies developed by the top management team" — the same executor/delivery framing as EOS's Integrator, reached independently, with academic rather than vendor provenance. Their research also found the role is *not* singular in shape — seven distinct COO archetypes exist (executor, heir apparent, change agent, co-leader, mentor, trusted partner, retention-MVP) depending on why the company created the seat — a genuine finding of variation, not a single template.

**Executive Producer (film/TV)** — Producers Guild of America's own Code of Credits (an industry authority, not one studio's marketing) states plainly: "The Line Producer/Co-Producer is the single individual who has the primary responsibility for the budget and logistics... All below-the-line department heads report to the Line Producer/Co-Producer." The Executive Producer sits above this chain, focused on financing/budget approval, "usually not involved in the actual filmmaking process... their financial role is their primary focus."

**Agency Producer (advertising)** — IPA (UK Institute of Practitioners in Advertising, industry body): "The Producer is responsible for taking the agreed creative idea and bringing the clients' wishes to life... manages the entire production process, from idea to execution, working in partnership with the client, creatives, account team... manages the budget for each project."

**Does the sequence "whole picture first, then decompose into functional directions" hold as real practice?** — **Confirmed for EOS and Scaling Up specifically, as their own documented process order** — both methodologies run Vision (EOS's V/TO; Scaling Up's One-Page Strategic Plan) *before* the functional Accountability Chart/FACe is built; the chart is explicitly drawn as depending on the vision already being settled. **Not confirmed the same way for the producer-type roles** (Executive Producer, agency producer): in both, the functional decomposition (department heads, line producer, production company structure) is an **inherited industry-standard structure**, not something the individual producer invents fresh each time by "seeing the whole picture" first — this is a real, non-smoothed difference between the two families of role, not a variant of the same finding. Worth naming honestly, not folded into a single confirmed answer.

**A second, more consequential nuance, found directly in the primary EOS material and not asked for by the brief but bearing directly on it**: the owner's own four-step description (sees whole business → decomposes → assigns specialists → is accountable for the financial result) maps, in real EOS practice, onto **two separate roles working together**, not one. EOS Worldwide's own Accountability Chart page states this as a design reason, not an incidental fact: "Most companies combine the Visionary and Integrator into one person... Most do not, because the skills required are fundamentally different." Gino Wickman's own writing quantifies how rare the combination is: "there is a rare person who actually possesses both skill sets of the Visionary and Integrator... about 1 out of 20 Visionaries (5%)." This is a real, sourced fact about the practice being modeled — not a design recommendation for the owner's agent (Responsibility Boundary Gate: this file states the fact, it does not decide whether the agent should split the role).

## 2. Delegation model — what's handed off, and how the result is checked

**Scaling Up names the mechanism most explicitly and generally**, independent of any single company's own jargon: "Successful delegation requires four components... 1. Pinpoint what the person or team needs to accomplish (Priorities). 2. Create a measurement system for monitoring progress (Data — qualitative and quantitative KPIs). 3. Provide feedback to the team or person (Meeting Rhythm). 4. Give appropriately timed recognition and reward." The same source draws an explicit, named line between delegation and its failure mode: "many leaders confuse delegation with abdication. Abdication is blindly handing over a task to someone with no formal feedback mechanism... all systems need a feedback loop, or they eventually drift out of control." This is a directly reusable distinction — see Card 2 below.

**EOS operationalizes the same four components under its own names**: the Accountability Chart assigns each seat 5–7 named "roles" (outcomes owned); Rocks (90-day priorities) are the concrete brief handed to a seat each quarter; the Scorecard is the measurable criterion (5–15 weekly numbers, each with a named owner, reviewed "on-track/off-track" with **no discussion** at the point of review — discussion is deliberately deferred to a separate step, see point 3); the weekly Level 10 Meeting is the feedback/check-in cadence. **Before any of that**, GWC (Gets it / Wants it / Capacity to do it — scored strictly yes/no, no partial credit) is the fit-check for whether a given person should hold a given seat at all — a distinct, prior question from "did they deliver the brief," closer to "is this the right specialist for this domain" than to task-level verification.

**Film production formalizes the same brief-plus-criterion pattern as a documented reporting chain, not a norm**: the Executive Producer's brief to the Line Producer is the approved budget and delivery schedule; the check is a recurring cost report ("a weekly report by the production accountant is required showing the amount expended, cost to complete" — troygould.com, describing standard completion-bond practice) — department heads report to the Line Producer, not directly to the EP, so the EP checks one aggregated report, not many individual ones.

**Agency production formalizes the client's own brief-plus-budget as the delegated unit**: "a savvy client will provide production budget parameters to their agency partners the minute they decide to launch a campaign... don't wait, ask this important question" (creedconsultingservices.com, an independent creative-operations-consultant account) — the producer is explicitly warned to actively seek the budget/criterion rather than assume it will be handed over, a real, named failure mode (criterion never actually fixed before work starts) worth noting as a parallel to this project's own pre-task-check discipline.

## 3. Financial accountability without direct control — mechanisms, not gut-feel

This is the strongest and most concrete material found in the whole search.

**EOS's answer is a substitution: leading indicators instead of the lagging P&L itself, plus a named escalation trigger.** The Integrator is described as accountable for "P&L results," but the actual weekly instrument is the Scorecard — deliberately built from *activity-based leading numbers*, not the P&L line items themselves, on the stated theory that "keeping the weekly activities of the team on pace ensures that the team will achieve its financial goals by the end of the quarter" (eosworldwide.com implementer blog). When a number goes off-track, the mechanism is explicit and has a named time threshold, sourced from an independent EOS-implementer practitioner: "If an important metric stays off for two to three weeks without action, it will likely remain that way... drop it to the Issues List and IDS (Identify, Discuss, Solve) the root cause" (integratorimpact.com). IDS itself is a fixed three-step method (Identify the real issue → Discuss openly → Solve with a named owner and a due-dated to-do) — escalation is not "raise it and hope," it is a structured procedure that must produce an owned action item, not a vague commitment ("we agreed to talk more about it" is explicitly named as a failure mode, not a valid Solve).

**Scaling Up's FACe operationalizes financial accountability as literal line-item ownership**, converting a lagging financial statement into named, individually-owned leading KPIs — the clearest "how do you make someone accountable for a number they don't personally generate" mechanism found: assign a person to each P&L/balance-sheet/cash-flow line, then derive the leading KPI that person actually controls day-to-day, rather than holding them to the lagging total itself.

**Film completion guaranty is the single most concrete, contractually-specified "accountable without control, not a guarantee, with a defined escalation ladder" mechanism found in this entire search** — worth reading closely because it is the closest external analog to the owner's own explicitly-decided "metric + control, not guarantee" framing, even though it comes from a completely different industry. A completion guarantor is a third party who is financially at risk if a film isn't finished and delivered on schedule, **without** having day-to-day creative or production control. The mechanism has three concrete, sourced features:
- **A defined contingency buffer before any intervention** — productions carry a standard ~10% budget contingency; "the vast majority of all films that are guarantied are completed within this 10 percent buffer," which is exactly why the guarantor "can frequently avoid the need to become more directly involved" (Vanderbilt JETLaw) — deviation within a known tolerance does not trigger escalation.
- **A weekly, structured reporting requirement as the trigger data** — "a weekly report by the production accountant is required showing the amount expended, cost to complete" (troygould.com) — the same "regular small check-ins surface deviation early" logic as EOS's weekly Scorecard, arrived at independently in a completely different domain.
- **A named three-tier escalation ladder, each tier more severe than the last, specified contractually in advance**: (1) compel the producer to complete and advance funds beyond budget if needed, (2) take over production directly, or (3) abandon and repay the financier's costs plus interest up to a pre-agreed liability cap (Vanderbilt JETLaw, cross-confirmed by `troygould.com` and the *Film Finance Inc v RBS* [2007] EWHC 195 judgment quoting an actual completion-guarantee contract's own text). The takeover right is real and contractually held but "rarely exercised" in practice (troygould.com) — the ladder's top tier functions as a backstop, not the default response.

**Cross-check across the three financial-accountability mechanisms above**: all three independently converge on the same underlying shape — *substitute a frequently-observable leading signal for the lagging outcome, define a tolerance band before anything counts as "off track," and attach a structured, staged, named-owner escalation procedure rather than either silence or an unstructured panic response.* This convergence, found across three genuinely unconnected source families (small-business operating-system coaching, general management theory applied to financial statements, and entertainment-industry contract law), is the strongest single finding in this file.

## 4. What transfers to the owner's current infrastructure, and what doesn't

Per the brief: the owner's real, already-proven mechanism is a file-based brief + a fixed-before-search/fixed-before-work criterion + a single-shot background AI-agent-specialist invocation (the same Engineer↔Researcher channel this very file is an instance of). This section states applicability only — it does not decide anything about the agent's design (Responsibility Boundary Gate: Research, not Design).

**Transfers — a pure information/process pattern, no new infrastructure required**:
- The whole-picture-then-decompose *sequence*, confirmed for EOS/Scaling Up specifically (point 1) — a vision/goal statement produced or confirmed before any functional breakdown is drawn — is a document-ordering convention, not a technical capability.
- Scaling Up's four-component delegation definition (priorities, measurement, feedback, recognition) and its named delegation-vs-abdication distinction map directly onto the already-used pattern of brief + fixed criterion + a check against that criterion by the party who issued the brief — the "abdication" failure mode (handing off work with no feedback loop) is a real, named risk worth checking any future design against, not a new capability to build.
- GWC's "is this the right specialist for this seat" fit-check is a conceptual pattern (a prior, explicit question distinct from "did the specialist deliver"), not an infrastructure requirement.
- The convergent financial-accountability shape from point 3 (leading indicator + tolerance band + staged named-owner escalation) is, structurally, a document/procedure design question, not an infrastructure one — it does not itself require any live data connection to describe or specify.

**Does not transfer without new infrastructure the owner does not currently have**:
- EOS's Scorecard and Scaling Up's FACe both assume **live, frequently-refreshed business data** (actual weekly sales/ops numbers, actual P&L feeds) flowing into the review mechanism — the owner's real estate business has no such live data integration into any agent today; this was already named as a gap in the 2026-08-17 investigation and is reconfirmed here from a different angle.
- The completion guarantor's three-tier intervention ladder presumes **real contractual and financial standing** — actual money at risk, actual legal authority to take over or advance funds — which an AI agent structurally cannot hold, and which the owner has already and separately decided against ("metric + control, not guarantee," not literal money-handling). The *shape* of the ladder (tolerance band → structured trigger → staged escalation) transfers as a pattern; the *contractual/financial substance* of it does not and was never proposed to.
- EOS's weekly Level 10 Meeting and IDS process assume a **standing, recurring, synchronous human leadership-team ritual** — the owner's current setup is asynchronous, file-based brief cycles, not a scheduled recurring meeting; adapting one into the other is a real design choice, not a fact this file can settle.
- Every human role examined in this file (Integrator, COO, Executive Producer, agency producer) is a **persistent, employed seat** with hiring/firing/compensation feedback loops built into how "right person, right seat" gets enforced over time (EOS's own People Analyzer explicitly ends in "keep, coach, or leave" as an outcome) — the AI-agent-specialist model is a per-task invocation with a brief, not a persistently staffed position with its own employment-style accountability loop. This structural difference is named here as a fact about the practice being compared against, not smoothed into "basically the same thing."

## Where sources genuinely disagree (not smoothed into a consensus)

**Whether this entire family of practice (EOS specifically) is substance or process theater is a real, live disagreement among people who have actually run it**, not a settled question this file can average away. Three real business owners on the `21hats` podcast, comparing lived experience: Laura Zander (used an implementer for years) concluded "it doesn't matter how good of a system you have if you don't have the right people... if you have the right people, you don't need a system"; Shawn Busse (consults across many EOS client companies but hasn't used it in his own) rates it "pretty simplistic," useful specifically for businesses wanting "consistent outputs" and explicitly *not* good for "product-market fit... marketing... culture" questions; Paul Downs (DIY, picked-and-chose) found the book's advice "aiming at a type of company... that's just way beyond where I'm ever going to get." An independent, critical book review (profit-strategies.com) makes a specific, structural charge: the vision/values in EOS's own process are "pushed down" top-down from the Visionary, not built with employee input — "Command and Control... if there is a real threat that this system doesn't hold up, this is where it would show." Against this, real named CEOs interviewed by `friedmanpartners.com` (Nesje, at T. Baker Smith) report the opposite experience in their own firm — genuine value from exactly the meeting-discipline and role-clarity structure the critics call thin. This is a real, unresolved disagreement between real practitioners about the same mechanism, not a gap in this search — reported as such.

## Reusable findings (Definition of Done 5a)

**Card 1 — Leading-indicator substitution + tolerance-band + staged escalation for financial accountability without direct control**
- **Назначение**: a three-part mechanism (observe a frequent leading signal, not the lagging outcome itself → define a tolerance band before anything counts as a deviation → run a structured, staged, named-owner escalation once the band is breached) for holding a coordinating role accountable for a financial result it does not directly execute.
- **Почему интересен**: found independently, with mechanism-level detail, in three unconnected source families (EOS Scorecard/IDS, Scaling Up FACe, film completion guaranty contract law) — genuine convergence, not one vendor's claim repeated.
- **Наш вывод**: адаптировать — the shape (leading signal + tolerance + staged escalation) is a sound external confirmation of a *pattern*, not a ready-made design.
- **Если адаптируем — с чем**: with the owner's existing file-based brief + criterion pattern and whatever financial-tracking mechanism the AI Business Producer's design eventually specifies — not decided here.
- **Почему именно так**: the pattern is proven across three genuinely independent domains, which is strong evidence the *shape* generalizes; but none of the three sources' actual live-data or contractual substance is available to the owner's agent today, so only the shape, not the implementation, is portable.
- **Что конкретно переносим**: ✔ leading-indicator-over-lagging-outcome framing, ✔ an explicit tolerance band before anything is treated as "off track," ✔ a structured, named-owner, due-dated escalation step (not silence, not an open-ended "monitor it"); ✘ any live data feed requirement, ✘ the completion guarantor's literal financial/contractual authority to take over or advance funds.

**Card 2 — Delegation-vs-abdication: the mandatory feedback loop**
- **Назначение**: an explicit, named distinction between delegation (priorities + measurement + feedback + recognition, all four present) and abdication (handing off work with no feedback mechanism at all).
- **Почему интересен**: stated directly and generally by an independent management-methodology source (Scaling Up/Rockefeller Habits), not tied to any one company's internal jargon — a clean, portable definition.
- **Наш вывод**: использовать полностью — as a check, not a redesign: any future brief-to-specialist handoff can be tested against these four components directly.
- **Если адаптируем/объединяем — с чем**: the already-proven Engineer↔Researcher brief-plus-fixed-criterion channel, which already has 3 of the 4 components (priorities/brief, measurement/criterion, feedback via the caller checking the result) — this card is a check that the fourth (recognition/reward) has no equivalent and may not need one for an AI-agent-specialist, not a gap that must be filled.
- **Почему именно так**: the four-component definition is simple enough to apply directly as a checklist without adaptation loss.
- **Что конкретно переносим**: ✔ the four-part definition as a review checklist for any future delegation design; ✘ nothing rejected — the whole card transfers as a check, not as an implementation.

**Card 3 — GWC-style seat-fit test, prior to and separate from task-level verification**
- **Назначение**: a strict yes/no fit-check (does the assignee understand the role, actually want it, and have the capacity for it) applied to *whether this specialist belongs on this seat at all*, kept explicitly separate from *whether this specific task was done well*.
- **Почему интересен**: names a real, distinct failure mode not covered by ordinary task-level verification — a capable generalist assigned to a domain-specific seat can pass every individual task check while being structurally the wrong fit for the seat over time.
- **Наш вывод**: отложить — worth naming now, not worth designing into anything yet; a fit-check for *AI-agent-specialists* (as opposed to employed humans) is a different problem than GWC was built for (no "wants it"/"capacity" in the human-motivational sense applies to an agent), and this file is not the place to redesign it.
- **Если адаптируем/объединяем — с чем**: not specified — genuinely a question for whoever designs the agent's specialist-assignment logic, if and when that becomes a real design question.
- **Почему именно так**: forcing an adaptation here would cross into Design, which this file's Responsibility Boundary Gate does not permit.
- **Что конкретно переносим**: ✔ the general principle (a seat-fit check is a distinct question from a task-outcome check, worth keeping separate); ✘ the specific GWC criteria themselves, which are built for human motivation/capacity and do not map onto an AI agent without real rework.
